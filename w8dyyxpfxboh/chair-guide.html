Building a department schedule

This guide takes a chair from raw files to a finished AY Memo. An AI tool writes the first draft. You fix it in the Schedule Editor, an AI checks your preferences, and you export the result. Plan on two to three working sessions.

1 Gather files→2 Plan sections→3 AI draft→4 Edit & fix→5 AI audit→6 Faculty review→7 Export AY Memo
Steps
  1. Before you start
  2. Gather the input files

0Before you start

  • Create one folder for the year, for example SCHEDULING/2027-2028/. Keep every file for that year in it.
  • Check the AY Memo due date on the Schedule of Classes Calendar of Deadlines. Work backward from it, and leave a week for faculty review.
  • Use a campus-approved AI tool (for example BoodleBox). Survey answers, names and loads are internal information. Do not paste them into a personal AI account.
  • Use Chrome or Edge on a computer with a mouse. Dragging cards in the editor does not work on touch screens.

Adding a classroom

Open hyland_classrooms.xlsx and add a row. Copy the format of the rows already there: room (written like HH2317), capacity, type and department assigned. The department column decides how the room is grouped in the editor: your rooms come first, shared rooms next, and every other room is marked "ask first." If you change the file while the editor is open, click Reload rooms… there.

Downloading enrolment from IRAP

Detailed instructions are coming. In short: download the raw per-semester file for each past term, keep the original file names, and save them all in one folder.

Running the survey

Send it early, at least three weeks before your draft date. Ask for days and times people can't teach, preferred teaching days, courses they want to teach, and any special needs, such as a room type or back-to-back sections. Export the responses to .xlsx.

2Decide how many sections

Turns enrolment into a number of in-person and online sections per course.

In the Section Planner, each course shows last year's sections and students, and a suggested number of sections at a target size, for Fall and Spring separately. Change the numbers wherever you know better: a new hire, a course moving online, or a rotation change in the long-range file. Save the result as section_plan_YYYY-YYYY.csv.

For a fall term that hasn't reached census yet, the IRAP numbers are low. Base the plan on the previous fall instead.

3Get an AI draft

The AI writes a first schedule in a fixed format that the editor can read.

  1. Open the Schedule Editor and click Draft format for AI. It downloads a file listing the required columns and rules.
  2. In your AI tool, upload that file together with last year's AY Memo, the rooms file, the long-range tab, the section plan, teaching loads, soft constraints and the survey responses.
  3. Paste the prompt below. Ask for the output as an .xlsx or .csv file.
You are drafting next year's course schedule for my department.

Files attached:
- Draft format: the exact columns and rules your output must follow. Follow it strictly.
- Last year's AY Memo: start from this pattern. Keep instructors in their usual days, times and rooms unless a rule below forces a change.
- Long-range rotation: offer only the courses listed for each term, in the modality listed.
- Section plan: the number of in-person and online sections per course per term.
- Teaching loads: the number of sections each instructor teaches in Fall and in Spring. Match these exactly.
- Rooms: use only these rooms, and never put a section in a room smaller than its cap. Prefer rooms owned by my department.
- Soft constraints and survey: respect them in priority order (High, then Medium, then Low).

Hard rules: no instructor or room double-booked; every in-person section has days, a time and a room; online sections have no room or time.

Output: one file in the draft format, one row per section. Then give a short list of every soft constraint or preference you could NOT meet, and why.

The AI's draft will have mistakes. Step 4 catches them.

4Fix it in the Schedule Editor

Here you clear every hard error. The checks run live as you edit.

  1. Open files… and select them all at once: the AI draft, hyland_classrooms.xlsx, the teaching loads, the section plan and the soft constraints. The editor recognises each file from its columns.
  2. Work through the Checks panel until there are no red errors: room clashes, instructor clashes, sections larger than their room, and in-person sections missing a time or room. Click a check to jump to the card.
  3. Then review the warnings: loads, section counts, and expected size per section.
  4. Verify the schedule from both sides:
    • Room view: is every room full but not double-booked, and are your own rooms used first?
    • Instructor view: does each person's week make sense, with no clashes, the right load and a reasonable spread? Online sections sit in the "Online / no time" column. Drag one to another row to reassign it.
  5. To move a card, drag it to an empty cell. Dropping it onto another card swaps the two. Click a card to edit it. Use Ctrl+Z to undo.
  6. Click Save schedule (.csv) often. This file is your working copy. The browser also keeps a draft, but it's only a backup.

5Ask AI to audit the soft rules

The AI checks the schedule and lists changes. It does not edit anything. You decide and make the changes.

  1. In the editor, click Export for AI audit (.xlsx). The file contains the schedule, your change log and the remaining warnings.
  2. Upload it to the AI tool with the soft constraints and survey responses, then paste the prompt below.
  3. Make the changes you agree with in the editor. Check that no red errors came back. Save.
  4. If you made many changes, run the audit again.
Audit this course schedule. Do NOT rewrite or return the schedule.

Attached: the schedule (with a change log and a list of remaining warnings), our soft constraints with priorities, and the faculty preference survey.

1. List every soft constraint and survey preference that the schedule breaks, highest priority first. For each one, give the constraint ID or the person, the sections involved, and why it is broken.
2. For each problem, suggest the smallest change that fixes it, for example "move FNBSLW 341-02 from TR 8:00 to TR 9:30 in the same room." Check that your change does not create a room or instructor clash or exceed a room's capacity.
3. Say which problems can't be fixed without breaking something more important.
4. End with a numbered to-do list of the changes I should make, in order.

6Faculty review

Send each instructor their sections, or share the instructor view, and give a short deadline. Make the corrections in the editor and save. This catches problems the survey missed, before the schedule is official.

7Export the new AY Memo

  1. Click Export AY Memo columns (.xlsx). It creates one sheet per term with the AY Memo columns.
  2. Paste each sheet into the official AY Memo template for the new year.
  3. Do a last check against the long-range rotation. Every course listed for each term should be in the schedule, in the listed modality.
  4. Submit before the deadline. Keep the final schedule_YYYY-YYYY.csv in the year folder. It becomes "last year's AY Memo" for next year.
